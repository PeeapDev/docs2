# Chapter 5 · Send the customer to pay

In Chapter 4 your server got back a `paymentUrl`. Now you put the customer in
front of it. Peeap hosts the whole payment experience — you don't build a card
form or a mobile-money flow yourself.

## Send them to the payment URL

The `paymentUrl` looks like:

```
https://checkout.peeap.com/popup.html?session=cs_3f9a8b2c1d4e5f60718293a4b5c6d7e8
```

How you get the customer there depends on your app:

<CodeGroup>

```javascript Browser redirect
// Simplest: full-page redirect to the hosted page
window.location.href = session.paymentUrl;
```

```html Link / button
<a href="https://checkout.peeap.com/popup.html?session=cs_...">
  Pay 50 SLE
</a>
```

```javascript Popup window
// Open in a popup and keep your page in the background
window.open(session.paymentUrl, "peeap_checkout", "width=480,height=720");
```

</CodeGroup>

<Note>
Your **server** created the session (Chapter 4) and handed the `paymentUrl` to
your page. Sending the browser to that URL is the one part that happens in the
customer's browser — that's expected and safe, because the URL only contains a
session id, not a key.
</Note>

## What the customer sees

The hosted page shows your **business name** and the amount, then offers the
payment methods you support:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Mobile money" icon="mobile">
    Orange Money / Africell Money via Monime. The customer approves on their phone.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Peeap card / wallet" icon="credit-card">
    Customers with a Peeap account pay straight from their balance.
  </Card>
  <Card title="QR code" icon="qrcode">
    The customer scans with the Peeap app to pay.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

You don't have to do anything to enable these — Peeap renders them. The customer
picks one, authorises, and Peeap collects the funds.

## Bringing the customer back

When you created the session you passed a `redirectUrl` (Chapter 4). After the
payment finishes, Peeap sends the customer's browser back to it with details
appended to the query string:

```
https://yourstore.com/payment/return
    ?status=success
    &session_id=cs_3f9a8b2c1d4e5f60718293a4b5c6d7e8
    &reference=order_1234
    &amount=50
    &currency=SLE
    &payment_method=mobile_money
```

So your return page can read `status`, `session_id`, and your own `reference` to
know which order this was.

<Warning>
**Do not trust the redirect.** The customer's browser controls that URL — anyone
can type `?status=success` by hand. The redirect is only a *hint* that the flow
finished. Before you mark the order paid or ship anything, your **server** must
independently confirm the session is really `COMPLETE`. That's exactly what
Chapter 6 does, and it's the single most important rule in this book.
</Warning>

<Tip>
**Advanced** — Treat the return page purely as UX: show a "thanks, we're
confirming your payment" spinner, then have it poll your own backend (which in
turn checks the session — Chapter 6) or wait for your webhook (Part 3) before
flipping the order to paid. Never let the browser's `?status=success` be the thing
that releases goods.
</Tip>

## If the customer abandons

If they close the page or never pay, nothing happens — the session simply
`EXPIRED`s after 30 minutes. No money moves and you owe nothing. Next time the
customer tries, create a brand-new session.

## Confirm it for real

The customer says they paid. Don't take their word — or their browser's. Continue
to [Chapter 6 → Confirm the payment](/guide/confirm-the-payment).
