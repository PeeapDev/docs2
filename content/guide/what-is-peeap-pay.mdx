# Chapter 1 · What is Peeap Pay

Before you write any code, get the mental model right. Most integration bugs come
from a wrong picture of who holds the money and when.

## The one-sentence version

> Peeap Pay lets your business collect money from a customer and have it land in
> your Peeap wallet — without you handling cards, mobile-money APIs, or bank
> rails yourself.

You make one API call. Peeap does the hard part.

## The four players

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="The customer" icon="user">
    The person paying. They pay with mobile money (Orange Money, Africell Money), a Peeap card, or by scanning a QR code in the Peeap app.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Your business (the merchant)" icon="store">
    You. You want money to arrive in your Peeap business wallet, and you want to know when it does.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Peeap" icon="building-columns">
    The network. It hosts the payment page, talks to the payment providers, moves the money, and tells you the result.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Monime" icon="network-wired">
    The rails underneath. Peeap uses Monime to reach the mobile-money operators. You never call Monime directly — Peeap does.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
**Advanced** — Think of Peeap the way you think of a card network: it's a wallet
and a settlement layer *on top of* the underlying mobile-money rails (Monime),
the same way Mastercard sits on top of banks. Your integration targets Peeap, and
Peeap abstracts whichever operator the customer actually used.
</Tip>

## How money moves

Here's the full lifecycle of a single payment. You'll implement each numbered
step over Chapters 4–6.

<Steps>
  <Step title="You create a checkout session">
    Your **server** tells Peeap "I want to collect 50 SLE for order #1234."
    Peeap creates a *session* and returns a payment link.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The customer opens the link">
    You send the customer to that link. They see a Peeap-hosted payment page with
    your business name on it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The customer pays">
    They choose mobile money, card, or QR and authorise the payment. Peeap (via
    Monime where needed) collects the funds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Peeap credits your wallet">
    On success, the money lands in your Peeap **business wallet**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You confirm and deliver">
    Your server confirms the session is `COMPLETE` (Chapter 6) — *then* you ship
    the goods or mark the order paid.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What a "session" is

A **checkout session** is a single attempt to collect a specific amount. It has:

- a unique id like `cs_3f9a8b...`
- an `amount` and `currency` (`SLE`)
- a `status` — starts at `OPEN`, becomes `COMPLETE` when paid, or `EXPIRED` after 30 minutes
- a payment link your customer opens

You create one session per payment. If a customer abandons checkout, that session
simply expires and you create a new one next time.

<Warning>
**A session is not money in your account.** Creating a session only means "I asked
to be paid." The money is yours only when the session is `COMPLETE`. Never deliver
goods on session *creation* — only on confirmed *completion*. Chapter 6 shows how,
and Part 3 explains why this rule has caused real money to get stuck in the past.
</Warning>

## Check your understanding

Before moving on, you should be able to answer:

1. Who creates the checkout session — the browser or your server? *(Your server.)*
2. When is the money actually yours? *(When the session status is `COMPLETE`.)*
3. Do you ever call Monime or an operator directly? *(No — Peeap does.)*
