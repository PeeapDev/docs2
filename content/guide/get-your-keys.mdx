# Chapter 3 · Get your keys

This is the only chapter where you click instead of code. At the end you'll have a
`pk_test_…` key — everything else in the book needs it.

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in to Peeap">
    Go to [my.peeap.com](https://my.peeap.com) and sign in (or create a free
    account).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Developer area">
    Navigate to **Merchant → Developer**, or go straight to
    [my.peeap.com/merchant/developer](https://my.peeap.com/merchant/developer).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a business">
    Click **Create Business**. Fill in your business details — name, category,
    website. The name you enter is what customers will see on the payment page, so
    use your real trading name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy your keys">
    As soon as the business is created, Peeap generates **four keys** for it:

    - `pk_test_…` and `sk_test_…` (test mode)
    - `pk_live_…` and `sk_live_…` (live mode)

    Copy the **test public key** (`pk_test_…`) — that's the one you'll use in
    Chapter 4.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
You don't need to wait for approval to get keys or to start building. A brand-new
business is in `PENDING` status, which is fine for unlimited **test** transactions
(see [Chapter 2](/guide/test-vs-live)).
</Note>

## Store your keys safely

Put your keys in an environment file, not in your source code:

```bash
# .env
PEEAP_PUBLIC_KEY=pk_test_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx
PEEAP_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx
```

Add `.env` to your `.gitignore` so it never gets committed.

<Warning>
The **secret** key (`sk_…`) must stay on your server. The **public** key (`pk_…`)
identifies your business when creating a session and is less sensitive, but still
keep both out of version control as a habit.
</Warning>

## Optional: set your return and webhook URLs

While you're in the dashboard, look for **Business Settings**. Two fields matter
later:

- **Callback / return URL** — where customers are sent after paying, if you don't
  specify one per payment. You can leave it blank for now and pass a `redirectUrl`
  on each checkout instead (Chapter 5).
- **Webhook URL** — where Peeap will POST payment events. We set this up in Part 3.
  Skip it for now.

## You're ready to build

You now have:

- ✅ A business in the dashboard
- ✅ A `pk_test_…` public key in your `.env`

That's everything you need for your first API call. Continue to
[Chapter 4 → Create a checkout](/guide/create-a-checkout).
