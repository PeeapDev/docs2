# Recipe · POS / QR scan-to-pay

At a counter or on a device, you display a QR code. A customer with the Peeap app
scans it and pays **wallet-to-wallet** — their Peeap balance to yours, instantly,
no mobile-money round-trip. This is the fastest settlement Peeap offers.

## What you implement vs. what the app does

This recipe has a neat division of labour:

| You (the merchant/POS) | The Peeap app (the customer) |
| --- | --- |
| Create a session, render its link as a QR, then **poll for completion**. | Scans the QR, authorises with PIN, and pays. |

You **do not** implement the payment itself — the customer's Peeap app handles
scanning, PIN entry, KYC/tier checks, and the wallet transfer. Your job is just
"ask for an amount, show a QR, wait for `COMPLETE`."

## 1. Create the session

Use the same authenticated create call as the storefront recipe — your `pk_…` key
identifies the business that will receive the funds:

```javascript
const r = await fetch("https://api.peeap.com/api/checkout/create", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
  body: JSON.stringify({
    publicKey: process.env.PEEAP_PUBLIC_KEY,
    amount: 75,
    currency: "SLE",
    description: "Counter sale",
    reference: "POS-2026-0001",
    metadata: { terminal_label: "POS-001", shift_id: "shift_42" },
  }),
});
const session = await r.json();
// session.paymentUrl → encode THIS as a QR
// session.sessionId  → poll THIS for completion
```

<Note>
**Why `/checkout/create` here too?** It authenticates with your public key and sets
the receiving business automatically, keeping POS consistent with your other
integrations. (A lower-level `POST /api/checkout/sessions` exists that takes a raw
`merchantId` and is what Peeap's own first-party apps use — prefer the
public-key path for your own integrations.)
</Note>

## 2. Render the QR

Encode `session.paymentUrl` as a QR code on your POS screen. Any QR library works —
the QR's content is simply that URL:

```javascript
import QRCode from "qrcode";
const dataUrl = await QRCode.toDataURL(session.paymentUrl);
// <img src={dataUrl} /> on the POS screen
```

The customer opens their Peeap app, scans, sees your **business name** and the
amount, enters their PIN, and confirms.

<Tip>
**Advanced — what happens on scan.** The Peeap app calls
`POST /api/checkout/sessions/{id}/scan-pay` with the payer's user id, wallet, and
PIN. Peeap then, atomically: rate-limits the attempt, validates the PIN, checks the
payer's KYC tier can *send* and your business can *receive* the amount, moves the
funds via the ledger (idempotency key `scan-pay-{sessionId}`), and marks the
session `COMPLETE`. You never call this endpoint — but knowing the gates helps you
explain a declined payment ("insufficient balance," "PIN required," "tier limit").
</Tip>

## 3. Poll for completion

Exactly like every other recipe — your source of truth is the session status:

```javascript
async function waitAtCounter(sessionId, { tries = 60, delayMs = 2000 } = {}) {
  for (let i = 0; i < tries; i++) {
    const s = await getSessionSafe(sessionId); // GET /api/checkout/sessions/{id}
    if (s.status === "COMPLETE") return "PAID";
    if (s.status === "EXPIRED") return "EXPIRED";
    await new Promise((res) => setTimeout(res, delayMs));
  }
  return "TIMEOUT";
}
```

When it returns `PAID`, show a success screen and print the receipt. Because the
money moved wallet-to-wallet, it's already in your business wallet — there's no
settlement lag to wait out.

<Warning>
Even at a counter, confirm on the **session status**, not on the customer showing
you their phone's "sent" screen. A screenshot isn't proof. Two seconds of polling
gives you the real answer.
</Warning>

## The complete POS loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cashier enters amount">
    Your POS calls `POST /api/checkout/create` → gets `paymentUrl` + `sessionId`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Screen shows the QR">
    Encode `paymentUrl` as a QR. The customer scans it with the Peeap app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Customer pays in-app">
    They confirm with their PIN; Peeap moves the funds and completes the session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="POS confirms and prints">
    Poll `GET /api/checkout/sessions/{id}`; on `COMPLETE`, print the receipt.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Hardware terminals

If you're integrating a physical device (e.g. a HEMI scan-to-pay speaker), pass
the device serial in metadata (`device_sn`) when creating the session — Peeap will
announce the successful payment through the device automatically on completion.
Everything else in this recipe is unchanged.

That completes the four recipes. Each is the same spine — create, pay, confirm by
polling, fulfil idempotently — adapted to a different surface.
